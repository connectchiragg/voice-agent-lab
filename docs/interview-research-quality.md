# Interview research quality

Acceptance checks:
- Resolve the employer from the invite domain and linked job description; discard namesakes.
- Explain product, customers and product versus consulting work.
- Report interviewer expertise only when supported by professional evidence.
- Include actual previous-round questions, follow-ups and agreed next steps where available.
- Keep transcript evidence distinct from automated coaching and recruiter statements.
- Date financial disclosures. Funding and share capital do not establish current cash/runway or salary budget.
- Use only role/location-relevant pay evidence; exclude generic country averages and aggregator estimates.
- Keep the spoken brief normally 80–140 words (160 maximum), with at most eight sourced notes, four practice questions and three prep actions. No forced coaching, research narration or repeated logistics.
- Surface company, interviewer, previous rounds and financial standing before extra facts.

Private-source retrieval checks the live encrypted transcript store and the current performance file, with provider summaries/transcript excerpts and the older evaluation export as fallbacks. Hidden recordings are excluded. Missing transcripts do not establish what was discussed or the outcome.

Validation uses fictional fixtures for ambiguous company names, question/answer retrieval, source checks, concise output, and direct language. Real-model replay uses existing private research inside the existing worker, without callbacks, calendar sync or notifications. Keep real interview data out of source control.

Lesson: prefer exact company identity and relevant evidence over adding more sources; more retrieved material made the previous brief less trustworthy when it mixed namesakes and generic pay data.
